How I work

My own route

I don’t start from the nearest template. I go back to what the problem really is and design the answer it needs, even when that looks different from what’s already out there.

Untangle first

Rule-heavy workflows, edge cases, systems nobody fully understands. I map them, find where people get stuck, and keep simplifying until the screen feels obvious.

Built for what’s next

I design AI experiences people can trust, and I prototype in React so ideas get tested as working things before engineering builds them.

People on the other end

Admins, students, recruiters, shoppers. I test with real users early and let what they actually do, not what we assume, decide what stays.

Designer at heart, entrepreneur by mindset. I take my own route to a problem, untangle it until it feels simple, and keep the people on the other end in mind the whole way.

Lakshmi Narayana on paper. Over three years designing B2C, B2B and SaaS products. Now designing AI automation for business schools at EDTEX, after building generative AI tools for enterprise teams at TensorGo.

How I got here

I studied Computer Science at IIIT RGUKT-Nuzvid, where I found that I enjoyed working out why an interface was confusing more than writing the code behind it. That curiosity turned into a career in understanding how people behave and turning it into products they can use without thinking.

Since then I’ve worked on everything from a news app to deep learning platforms. The range keeps me versatile, and the engineering background means I can talk constraints with developers instead of handing over screens and hoping.

What I believe

Good design sits where user needs, business goals and technical reality meet.

  • Break a complex problem down to its core before reaching for patterns.

  • A design choice should be explainable in terms of what it does for the product.

  • The best answers come from working closely with PMs, engineers and the people using the product.

  • Accessible, inclusive work isn’t a final checklist. It shapes the design from the start.

How I’m wired

I’m an Aquarian, and it probably shows. I’d rather find my own way to a solution than copy the obvious one. When a system confuses people, I get curious about why, and I keep pulling at it until it makes sense.

I’m always looking at what’s next, especially in AI. But the question I come back to is simple: is this fair and useful for the person using it?

Designing AI teammates that join the meeting

TensorGo’s HumAIns are AI teammates that join Zoom, Meet and Teams calls to interview candidates, coach sales teams and turn meetings into decisions. I designed how people set them up, work alongside them and trust what they deliver.

Company
TensorGo
Role
Product designer
Timeline
Feb 2024 to Sep 2025
Focus
Agentic AI, B2B SaaS

TensorGo is a Gartner Cool Vendor in AI and won Oracle’s GenAI Innovation Award 2025 (JAPAC).

The challenge

When software acts on its own, the design questions change. When should the AI speak, and when should it stay quiet? How does a recruiter know a score is fair? What does a sales rep need in the two seconds before answering an objection?

My job was to make an autonomous teammate feel predictable, accountable and easy to direct, for recruiters, sales leaders and managers who just need it to work.

My contributions

  • Setting up a teammate

    Giving an AI teammate its role, knowledge, tone and guardrails without code in Voice Studio, then testing it in practice calls before it meets real people.

  • The AI interviewer

    From identity check to a structured scorecard: an interview candidates can trust and recruiters can compare at a glance.

  • The meeting teammate

    Connect a calendar, invite the teammate, and get minutes, decisions and action items back when the call ends.

  • Live sales nudges

    A quiet panel beside the call that offers the next question or objection response, without pulling the rep out of the conversation.

  • Knowledge and platform

    HumAIn Brain, hyperspace and the developer APIs: where teams give the AI what it should know, control who sees it and watch how it performs.

  • Talk to Nia

    The live demo on the website. A short form, a fair waiting room, a real conversation with an AI teammate, and clear recovery when a session can’t connect.

How I design for agents

  • Show what the AI is doing

    Clear states like joining, listening and preparing a report replace guesswork about what the agent is up to.

  • Keep people in charge

    An agent goes live only after someone approves it, and its behaviour stays editable afterwards.

  • Make the output traceable

    Scores, summaries and answers point back to where they came from, so people can check them before acting.

  • Design the failures too

    When an agent can’t join or a session drops, say what happened and offer the next step.
